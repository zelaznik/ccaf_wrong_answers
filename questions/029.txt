SCENARIO: You are building a multi-agent research system using the Claude Agent SDK. A coordinator agent delegates to specialized subagents: one searches the web, one analyzes documents, one synthesizes findings, and one generates reports. The system researches topics and produces comprehensive, cited reports.

QUESTION: During QA review of a report the system produced on global solar capacity growth, a reviewer notices that the final report states a single capacity figure as established fact. Inspecting the web search subagent's raw output, they find two credible sources with materially different figures, published two years apart. The summaries the synthesis agent received were prose paragraphs containing neither source names nor publication dates, and the synthesis agent silently included only one of the two figures. What change most effectively addresses this failure?
Add an independent review instance that re-checks the report and replaces any disputed statistic with the value that appears in the majority of sources.
Explanation
Majority voting treats prevalence as correctness, which fails when a widely repeated figure originates from a single outdated report. It also still produces a single unattributed number, so readers cannot see the disagreement or evaluate the competing sources themselves.
Your answer is incorrect
Instruct the synthesis agent to resolve statistical conflicts automatically by always adopting the figure from the most recently published source.
Explanation
Automatic recency-based selection still hides the disagreement from readers and assumes newer always means more accurate, which is not reliable across differing methodologies or measurement periods. The documented practice is to annotate conflicts with source attribution rather than arbitrarily selecting one value.
Restrict the web search subagent to a curated allowlist containing one authoritative source per topic so conflicting statistics never enter the pipeline.
Explanation
Limiting research to a single source per topic undermines the system's purpose of producing comprehensive reports and simply hides disagreement rather than handling it. Credible sources legitimately differ, and the report should represent that uncertainty rather than engineer it away by narrowing coverage.
Correct answer
Require subagents to emit structured claim-source records with publication dates, and have the synthesis agent surface conflicting figures with attribution.
Explanation
This preserves provenance through the synthesis step so conflicts remain visible and attributable rather than silently resolved by the synthesis agent. Publication dates also allow the system to distinguish genuine disagreement from temporal differences between sources, and surfacing annotated conflicts lets the coordinator or a human decide how to reconcile them instead of the synthesis agent silently choosing.
Overall explanation
<p>The root failure here is that <b>source attribution was lost during summarization</b>: once findings were compressed into prose without source names or dates, the synthesis agent had no way to recognize that two figures came from different sources published at different times, so it silently picked one. The fix must operate on the data contract between agents, not on the synthesis agent's judgment alone. Requiring each subagent to emit <b>claim-source mappings</b> (claim, evidence excerpt, source identifier, publication date) as structured output means provenance survives every downstream combination step, and the synthesis agent can be instructed to <i>annotate</i> conflicts with attribution instead of resolving them unilaterally.</p><p>Publication dates matter for a subtle reason: figures measured two years apart are often not contradictions at all, just snapshots of a changing quantity. Without temporal metadata, a synthesis agent either misreports one snapshot as the current fact or flags a false conflict. With dates attached, the coordinator (or a human reviewer) can decide whether the values reflect growth over time or genuine methodological disagreement.</p><p>The distractors all share a common flaw: they collapse uncertainty into a single unattributed number. Recency-based selection and majority voting are automatic reconciliation heuristics that hide disagreement and can amplify errors (a stale figure repeated by many outlets wins a majority vote). Restricting search to one source per topic avoids conflicts only by sacrificing the comprehensiveness the system exists to provide. This aligns with Anthropic's broader reliability guidance: require citations for claims, allow the model to express uncertainty, and validate critical information rather than presenting unverified values as fact. For document-grounded pipelines, the API's built-in <a href="https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations">Citations</a> feature provides verifiable passage-level attribution, and <a href="https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations">Reduce hallucinations</a> covers grounding claims in quoted, cited evidence.</p>
